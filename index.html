Conclusion

Under the strict matched-policy benchmark, the best current StructSplat idea is better than the existing-policy analogues on PSNR and convergence. It is not the best by MS-SSIM: the GaussianImage++/GaussianImage-style analogues score slightly higher there in this small run.

Read this as directional, not final-paper proof. It is 4 images, 2 seeds, 160px, 640 Gaussians, 500 iterations. The result is strong enough to say the StructSplat tensor/feature-cap idea is promising and currently wins the controlled PSNR/AUC test. It is not enough to claim universal superiority.

Fairness Protocol

  • Images: 4 pinned COCO fixtures, resized to max side 160
  • Budget: growth rows start at 320, all rows end at 640 Gaussians
  • Growth: 5 waves for growth methods; GaussianImage fixed starts full-count and does not grow
  • Optimization: 500 iterations, L1 pixel loss, SSIM weight 0.3
  • Runtime: renderer cuda, device cuda, render chunk 4,096
  • Randomness: seeds 0, 1; every method sees the same image/seed cells
Native GaussianImage++, Image-GS, and Instant-GI repos/checkpoints are not installed locally. Their rows here are matched-policy analogues inside the StructSplat harness, which is the fair way to isolate policy differences. Native GaussianImage-RS and AIR are shown separately below.
